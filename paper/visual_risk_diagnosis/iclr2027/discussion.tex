\section{Supporting controls and relation to earlier work}
\label{sec:discussion}
\paragraph{Learning and decision-rule effects remain distinct.}
A prior matched geometry $\times $ source-raster experiment trained 20 models at
equal budgets. Independent geometry improves new-appearance regret relative to
the balanced-anchor scheme at both source 48 and source 64, with crossed 95\%
intervals [.003101,.006853] and[.002850,.017690], each exceeding the predeclared
.001 effect threshold. This controls a genuine learning-related effect while
keeping common-reference scoring fixed. The intervention changes shape, size,
rotation, and position jointly; it does not isolate position alone.

Earlier fixed-weight and risk-control results are summarized in
Table~\ref{tab:support}; details are in Appendix~\ref{app:earlier}. They support
the argument without defining its novelty. E1 uses historical checkpoints and
consumed diagnostic data. E3 uses the five E2 independent/source 64 models with
1,600 new calibration and 2,000 test scenes per domain. Their residual and
certification results must not be treated as one within-model factorial test.

Joint protection and selective-risk control protect different objects. If
nonnegative estimates have a joint residual quantile $ q>\tau $, adding that bound
necessarily rejects all actions. A rule controlling risk among accepted actions
need not protect every unselected candidate. The E3 exact-binomial finite-bank
procedure applies established Learn then Test principles~\citep{ltt}; its
success shows that an imperfect pipeline can be useful under a specified rule
and distribution. It neither validates its measurement interface nor supplies
certificates for modified aggregation.

\paragraph{Reconstruction accuracy and learned acceptance.}
Volume-of-fluid methods recover material interfaces from cell fractions;
\citet{pilliod2004} compare reconstruction accuracy, including the first-order
Parker--Youngs method. Their setting establishes that neighboring fractions
can support a better spatial interpretation. Our remaining question is what
that intervention does after replacing exact fractions with learned ones and
passing the resulting scores through action acceptance. The exact-target and
learned threshold curves separate these two empirical outcomes in one task;
they do not characterize the VOF method family.

\paragraph{Consistent risk representations and error attribution.}
Lambda-Field constructs an occupancy representation and path-risk computation
consistent with its collision-event model~\citep{lambda2019}. Our exposure law
is different and held fixed. The remaining diagnostic question is how much of
a given pipeline's cost error comes from fitting the target, rasterizing its
source, or aggregating it. Common-footprint oracle replacements answer this
within the case. They neither test Lambda-Field under its own risk definition
nor supply a new physical-risk representation.

\paragraph{Task-oriented design and fixed-pipeline diagnosis.}
Task-based quantization studies downstream estimation with limited-rate
representations and joint designs~\citep{shlezinger2019}; Smart Predict, then
Optimize relates prediction to decision loss~\citep{spo}. Our coarse fractions
are spatial averages, not that finite-bit quantization model. We isolate a
specific spatial error and its interaction with learned errors under fixed
action queries. The empirical contribution is an attributable case with
observed gains and harms, not a task-sufficiency theorem or decoder-choice rule.
Egocentric Conformal Prediction and Conformal Decision Theory address
decision-relevant uncertainty and loss~\citep{egocentric,cdt}. Their perspective
also motivates keeping the validity of a measurement separate from the object
protected by a risk-control procedure.

\section{Limitations and conclusion}
\label{sec:limitations}
The task is synthetic, uses a known cost law, and restricts actions to four
candidates. Numerical checks condition on a fixed footprint and do not prove
continuum accuracy. Oracle boundary features are privileged diagnostics. The
prospective confirmation concerns the original mechanism; the added reconstruction
and shift are retrospective comparisons on consumed data. Five model seeds limit
training-distribution inference. A single first-order PLIC baseline cannot
characterize every same-information decoder, and successful oracle-input repair
would not by itself establish safe learned-model deployment.

An attempted real-image extension using RELLIS-3D~\citep{rellis} stopped before
mechanism evaluation because the geometric reference lacked independent error
bounds. Its initial 24-frame screen and subsequent prerequisite stop are retained
in the appendix; the planned new 48-frame set was never instantiated. It supplies
neither supporting nor refuting evidence for cross-task transfer. The extension
remains closed; no VLM, RL or external experiment is added.

This controlled case separates learning error from residual measurement error
under a fixed spatial target and cost law. The latter has actual conservative
decision consequences that survive a prospective numerical check. Same-information
reconstruction recovers some opportunities but adds danger; its clearer exact-target
gains do not establish a stable learned advantage across equal empirical risk
ceilings. Event-level error opposition explains individual transitions without
providing a causal share or prospective diagnostic. The resulting knowledge is
a pipeline attribution and its measured boundaries. A general principle for
choosing spatial decoders remains beyond the evidence.
