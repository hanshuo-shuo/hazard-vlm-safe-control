\section{Same-information interventions and their limits}
\label{sec:comparison}
The oracle replacement fixes learning error while retaining the aggregator.
We now intervene on spatial interpretation. Development uses the consumed
2,000-scene IID discovery bank; evaluation appends to the already consumed
2,000-scene confirmation bank. All five learned models remain fixed.

\paragraph{Inputs and first comparison.}
Perfect target 64 and learned fields are examined separately. Uniform
integration and the established Parker--Youngs PLIC reconstruction
\citep{pilliod2004} receive identical coarse fields and F512 footprints.
PLIC estimates a cell normal from a fixed weighted $3\times3$ coarse gradient
and analytically places a half-plane matching the original fraction. Empty and
full cells remain unchanged; gradient L1 norm at most $10^{-12}$ retains uniform
occupancy. Exact fine-square fractions are integrated against F512. The routine
receives no true patch parameters, fine property masks, or oracle normals.
Appendix~\ref{app:shift} records the original scalar-offset comparison: its
 development unsafe-count constraint was not imposed on PLIC. That selected
 offset is insufficient to represent the entire scalar-adjustment family.

At the unchanged acceptance threshold .02, PLIC recovers 44 of the perfect
 target's 54 lost safe opportunities and introduces 16 unsafe accepts, with no
 new safe losses. Conditional unsafe frequency rises from .13\% to 1.13\% and
 opportunity use from 96.61\% to 99.37\%. For learned fields, it recovers 83
 model--scene decisions and adds 43 unsafe accepts: $R$ rises from 1.03\% to
 1.56\% and $\eta$ from 96.77\% to 97.81\%. These gains and harms survive fixing
 the uniform-selected action in aggregate; individual action changes remain recorded.

The newly accepted subsets have unsafe fractions $16/(44+16)=26.7\%$ for
 perfect targets and $43/(83+43)=34.1\%$ for learned fields. These retrospective
 subset descriptions are distinct from risk among all accepts and from the
 earlier 5\% requirement. They show how large existing safe-acceptance counts
 can coexist with much higher danger in marginally added decisions.

\paragraph{Identical empirical risk ceilings.}
A global translation of the uniform score is equivalent to changing its
 acceptance threshold. We therefore compare both complete threshold families,
 including every distinct score with tied scores entering together. The true
 danger definition remains $\refcost>.02$. The primary analysis fixes the
 uniform-selected action and truth for both scores. Each-operator selection is
 a secondary analysis. All five learned models share a scalar threshold in the
 pooled comparison; each model's curve is also retained.

At a ceiling $\beta$, each score uses exactly the same rule: maximize safe
 acceptance among all thresholds with empirical $R\le\beta$; ties prefer fewer
 unsafe accepts, then the smaller threshold. Every prefix is examined because
 conditional risk need not grow monotonically with threshold. Reject-all has
 undefined risk. Figure~\ref{fig:matched} shows the resulting empirical envelopes;
 the full raw paths are preserved. Outcome labels select these points on each
 consumed bank. They characterize observed score discrimination, not deployable
 thresholds with guaranteed risk. No randomization interpolates an exact risk match.

Exact-target gains are clearest at low risk ceilings. At $\beta=1\%$, appended
 $\eta$ is 98.43\% for uniform scores and 99.31\% for PLIC, a .88 pp difference
 with descriptive interval [.31,1.82]. On learned fields the corresponding values
 are 96.66\% and 97.11\%, a .45 pp difference [-.13,1.49]. At 2\% the learned
 difference is .30 pp [-.19,.66]; at 5\% it is .18 pp [.00,.38]. Learned point
 estimates are positive at all eight listed ceilings, but intervals cross zero
 throughout .5--4\%, on both banks. This does not establish equivalence or that
 all recovery is a scalar effect. It does not establish a stable learned advantage
 over scalar threshold adjustment either.

A continuation screen was fixed before computing these full curves, while
 acknowledging that both banks were already consumed. It required a nontrivial
 positive learned difference across a contiguous risk range, uncertainty support,
 and consistency across models and banks (Appendix~\ref{app:matched}). It fails.
 No additional scene, model, or reconstruction is evaluated. Development-selected
 thresholds applied unchanged to the consumed appended bank are also retained;
 their actual risks, rather than their development ceilings, describe that transfer.

\paragraph{A retrospective cancellation pattern.}
Among the 43 newly unsafe learned decisions at threshold .02, 25 keep the
 action, start with opposing model-relative and target-measurement errors,
 improve absolute measurement error, and worsen absolute total error.
Table~\ref{tab:cancellation} gives the first matching event in scene/model order.
Both component absolute errors decrease in this example while total
 underestimation grows. All 43 events, including the other 18, are retained.

This is an outcome-conditioned pattern, not the probability of failure among
 all initially opposed-error actions. Changing the aggregator changes both
 error terms; they are not independently intervened modules. The evidence
 establishes deterministic event-level coexistence of component improvement
 and decision harm, without identifying causal shares or a predictor of future
 failure. Together, these controls restrict the repair interpretation of the
 original target--aggregation loss. Modified scores inherit no earlier risk certificate.
